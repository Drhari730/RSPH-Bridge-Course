## Overview & Pedagogical Dual-Track Philosophy

This bridge curriculum unites two complementary lenses of healthcare:
- **The Public Health Lens (Population-Level):** Prevention, health determinants, surveillance, community engagement, disease burden, and equity.
- **The Healthcare Management Lens (Institutional/Facility-Level):** Operational effectiveness, quality of care, resource allocation, team dynamics, leadership in crisis, and organizational stewardship.

By learning these foundations jointly, **MPH students understand the hospital as an essential tertiary hub of public health**, and **MHA students understand community health determinants that drive patient inflow and clinical outcomes**.

# MODULE 1: Introduction to Public Health & Health Systems

- **Target Hours:** 12 Hours (6 Hours Didactic / Interactive Lectures + 6 Hours Case Discussions & Problem-Based Learning)
- **Primary Courses Mapped:**
  - MPH: `PHC501A` (Principles & Practice of Public Health) & `PHC502A` (Public Health Systems & Health Policy)
  - MHA: `HAC504C` (Epidemiology and Public Health Administration - Units 3, 4, 5, 6)
- **Essential Readings:**
  - *Park's Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine (28th Ed.)* – Chapters on Concept of Health and Disease, Healthcare Delivery System in India.
  - *Oxford Textbook of Global Public Health (7th Ed.)* – The Scope and Mandate of Public Health.
  - *IPHS 2022 Guidelines* (MoHFW) – Sub-Centre / HWC, PHC, CHC, and Sub-District/District Hospitals.

### Learning Outcomes (COs)
At the end of this module, the student will be able to:
1. Distinguish between clinical care (individual focus) and public health (population focus) and apply the **Iceberg Phenomenon of Disease**.
2. Analyze the **Social, Environmental, and Commercial Determinants of Health** and explain why healthcare facilities alone cannot solve health inequalities.
3. Classify health interventions across the **Four Levels of Prevention** (Primordial, Primary, Secondary, Tertiary).
4. Describe the **three-tier healthcare delivery system of India** and evaluate adherence to **IPHS 2022 Norms**.
5. Explain the operational mechanisms of **Ayushman Bharat** (AB-HWCs / Ayushman Arogya Mandir and PM-JAY) and their public-private hospital touchpoints.

---

### Session-by-Session Breakdown (Module 1)

#### Session 1.1: The Paradigm Shift — Medicine vs. Public Health (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Evolution of public health: From sanitation and quarantine to social justice, planetary health, and health equity.
  - WHO definition of health; Spectrum of health; The **Iceberg Phenomenon of Disease** (diagnosed cases vs. subclinical/unreached community reservoir).
  - Public health core functions: Assessment, Policy Development, and Assurance.
- **Interactive Exercise:**
  - *The Clinical vs. Population Matrix:* Students analyze Diabetes Mellitus Type II—as an endocrinologist treating an individual patient vs. as a public health administrator managing population risk factors (dietary transition, urban planning, screening camps).

#### Session 1.2: Determinants of Health & Health Inequity (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - The Dahlgren-Whitehead rainbow model of health determinants.
  - Social determinants: Education, gender, caste, socioeconomic status, sanitation, housing.
  - Health equality vs. Health equity (the concept of horizontal and vertical equity).
  - Out-of-pocket expenditure (OOPE) and catastrophic health expenditures in India.
- **Reading Assignment:**
  - Devaki Nambiar (2017) / Park PSM: Chapter on *Social Sciences and Health*.

#### Session 1.3: Disease Prevention & Levels of Intervention (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Natural history of disease: Pre-pathogenesis phase $\rightarrow$ Pathogenesis phase $\rightarrow$ Recovery/Disability/Death.
  - The 4 Levels of Prevention & Modes of Intervention:
    - *Primordial:* Preventing the emergence of risk factors (e.g., national tobacco tax policies).
    - *Primary:* Health promotion & specific protection (e.g., immunization, food fortification).
    - *Secondary:* Early diagnosis and prompt treatment (e.g., Pap smears, BP screening at HWCs).
    - *Tertiary:* Disability limitation & rehabilitation (e.g., post-stroke rehab, diabetic foot care).
- **Group Drill:**
  - Mapping cervical cancer interventions across the 4 levels of prevention.

#### Session 1.4: The Indian Health System Architecture & IPHS 2022 (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Constitutional provisions (Health as a State subject; Concurrent areas; 73rd/74th Constitutional Amendments).
  - Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary infrastructure in India:
    - *Sub-Health Centre (SHC) / Ayushman Arogya Mandir:* Staffing (CHO, ANM, MPW, ASHA), population norms (3,000–5,000).
    - *Primary Health Centre (PHC):* Staffing, basic lab, emergency obstetric care, population norms (20,000–30,000).
    - *Community Health Centre (CHC):* 30 beds, first referral unit (FRU), 4 core specialists (Medicine, Surgery, OBGYN, Paediatrics).
    - *Sub-District & District Hospitals (DH):* 100–500 beds, secondary/tertiary referral, ICU, blood bank.
    - *Tertiary Care:* Medical colleges, AIIMS, Autonomous institutes.
- **Resource Reference:**
  - MoHFW *Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS) 2022 Revised Guidelines*.

#### Session 1.5: National Health Mission & Ayushman Bharat (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - National Health Mission (NRHM & NUHM components; Communitization, Rogi Kalyan Samiti, Untied Funds).
  - Major vertical disease control programmes: National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP), National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP), National Programme for Non-Communicable Diseases (NP-NCD).
  - Ayushman Bharat:
    - Pillar 1: Comprehensive Primary Health Care via 1.5+ Lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (12 expanded service packages).
    - Pillar 2: Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) tertiary insurance (₹5 Lakhs/family/year for secondary & tertiary hospitalization).
  - Hospital empannelment under PM-JAY and referral mechanisms.

#### Session 1.6: Seminar & Applied Case Workshop (2 Hours)
- **Case Study 1:** *Bridging the Clinic and the Community.*
  - Scenario: A 300-bed private teaching hospital in Bengaluru witnesses an unexplained 300% surge in pediatric acute diarrheal admissions from an urban informal settlement.
  - Task: Form 4 student groups (mixed MPH + MHA). Group A outlines hospital admission surge capacity, bed triaging, and IV fluid supply logistics; Group B outlines the epidemiological field investigation, water-source testing, and municipal liaison.

# MODULE 2: Healthcare Management, Organizational Behaviour & Leadership

- **Target Hours:** 12 Hours (6 Hours Didactic / Interactive Lectures + 6 Hours Case Studies, Role-Plays & Simulations)
- **Primary Courses Mapped:**
  - MPH: `PHC504B` (Healthcare Management and Leadership)
  - MHA: `HAC501C` (Organizational Behavior & Fundamentals of Management) & `HAC510C` (Healthcare Management and Leadership)
- **Essential Readings:**
  - *Edmonstone (2019). Systems Leadership in Health and Social Care (Routledge).*
  - *Lee (2018). HBR's 10 Must Reads on Leadership for Healthcare (Harvard Business Review Press).*
  - *Galer, Vriesendorp, Ellis (2005). Managers Who Lead: A Handbook for Improving Health Services (MSH).*

### Learning Outcomes (COs)
At the end of this module, the student will be able to:
1. Explain classical and modern **management functions (POSDCORB)** applied to healthcare organizations and public health programs.
2. Differentiate between **Management (doing things right)** and **Leadership (doing the right things)** in complex, high-stakes health environments.
3. Apply behavioral science and **motivation theories** (Maslow, Herzberg) to healthcare workforce retention and productivity.
4. Assess personal leadership and conflict-resolution styles using validated frameworks (Situational Leadership, Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode).
5. Formulate strategies for **change management** and **crisis leadership** during public health emergencies and hospital workflow disruptions.

---

### Session-by-Session Breakdown (Module 2)

#### Session 2.1: Healthcare Organizations as Complex Adaptive Systems (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - What makes healthcare organizations unique? High ambiguity, dual hierarchy (clinical autonomy vs. administrative governance), ethical imperatives, and 24/7 operations.
  - Organizational structure: Functional, Matrix, Flat vs. Tall, Departmental silos.
  - Organizational culture and climate: Psychological safety, culture of safety, patient-centered care.
  - Systems thinking in health: Feedback loops, unintended consequences of top-down policies.

#### Session 2.2: Classical Management Functions in Healthcare (POSDCORB) (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - **Planning:** Strategic, tactical, and operational planning; SMART objectives; Gantt charts; SWOT analysis in healthcare.
  - **Organizing:** Resource grouping, delegation of authority, span of control, standard operating procedures (SOPs).
  - **Staffing & Directing:** Workload indicators of staffing need (WISN), orientation, supervision vs. policing.
  - **Coordinating & Budgeting:** Line-item budgets, capital vs. operational expenditure (CAPEX vs. OPEX), performance auditing.
- **Practical Exercise:**
  - Develop a high-level 30-day operational plan to establish an Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) Committee in a newly opened 100-bed hospital.

#### Session 2.3: Healthcare Leadership Theories & Frameworks (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Evolution of leadership thought: Trait theory $\rightarrow$ Behavioral theory $\rightarrow$ Contingency theory $\rightarrow$ Transformational leadership.
  - **Hersey-Blanchard Situational Leadership Model:** Directing (S1), Coaching (S2), Supporting (S3), Delegating (S4) matched to follower readiness (R1–R4).
  - The Ruth-Seliger Model and Maxwell’s 5 Levels of Leadership (Position, Permission, Production, People Development, Pinnacle).
  - Subroto Bagchi’s model: *Platform vs. Purpose* and legitimate sources of power (expert, referent, legitimate, reward, coercive).

#### Session 2.4: Human Motivation & Team Dynamics in Healthcare (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Why healthcare staff burn out: Dual stressors, emotional exhaustion, moral injury.
  - Motivation theories in health:
    - Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs (basic security to self-actualization).
    - Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory (Hygiene factors vs. Motivators: Why money prevents dissatisfaction but recognition and growth drive performance).
    - Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic motivation.
  - Team Dynamics: Tuckman’s Stages of Team Development (Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing, Adjourning).
  - Building high-reliability multidisciplinary teams (doctors, nurses, epidemiologists, administrators, community workers).

#### Session 2.5: Conflict Management, Negotiation & Communication (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Sources of conflict in healthcare (resource scarcity, clinical vs. cost trade-offs, interpersonal friction, role ambiguity).
  - **Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument (TKI):** Competing, Collaborating, Compromising, Avoiding, Accommodating.
  - Communicating under pressure: SBAR technique (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) for clinical and administrative handoffs.
  - Crisis communication with media, patient relatives, and regulatory authorities.

#### Session 2.6: Change Management & Crisis Leadership Simulation (2 Hours)
- **Topics Covered:**
  - Leading in VUCA environments (Volatility, Uncertainty, Complexity, Ambiguity).
  - Kotter’s 8-Step Change Model applied to health (e.g., implementing an electronic medical record / EMR system).
  - Crisis leadership principles: Decisiveness without complete information, transparency, emotional containment.
- **Capstone Simulation Exercise (Joint MPH + MHA):**
  - *Simulation:* A sudden fire breaks out in the hospital pharmacy, coupled with a simultaneous admission of 40 mass casualty victims from an industrial blast.
  - *Debrief:* Roles assigned—Incident Commander, Chief Medical Officer, Public Health Liaison Officer, Operations Chief, Public Relations Officer.
