# MPH & MHA Bridge Course: Diagnostic Pre-Test & Assessment Package
**Ramaiah School of Public Health (RSPH) / Faculty of Life & Allied Health Sciences, MSRUAS**  
*Purpose:* Administered on Day 1 as a non-punitive baseline diagnostic evaluation to profile incoming students across clinical and non-clinical backgrounds; re-administered on the final day as a post-test to quantify competency gains.

---

## Assessment Matrix & Competency Domains

The assessment consists of **30 Multiple Choice Questions (1 mark each; 45 minutes)** divided evenly across the 5 bridge pillars:

| Domain | Focus Area | Questions | Target Competency |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **I** | Introduction to Public Health & Health Systems | Q1 – Q6 | Health definitions, determinants, prevention levels, Indian healthcare tiering (IPHS). |
| **II** | Healthcare Management & Leadership | Q7 – Q12 | Management functions (POSDCORB), organizational behavior, motivation, leadership models. |
| **III** | Foundations of Epidemiology | Q13 – Q18 | Disease transmission, rates vs proportions, study designs, screening metrics ($2 \times 2$ table). |
| **IV** | Biostatistics & Quantitative Thinking | Q19 – Q24 | Scales of measurement, central tendency & dispersion, normal curve, hypothesis testing, $p$-values. |
| **V** | Research Methodology & Bioethics | Q25 – Q30 | PICO question formulation, literature searching, Vancouver referencing, bioethical principles, IEC review. |

## Diagnostic Test Instrument (30 MCQs)

### Domain I: Public Health & Health Systems

**Q1. According to the WHO definition, which of the following is TRUE regarding the concept of health?**  
A. Health is solely the objective absence of disease or infirmity.  
B. Health is a static state achieved only in the absence of clinical symptoms.  
C. Health is a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease.  
D. Health is defined strictly by biological markers within normal laboratory reference ranges.

**Q2. In the "Iceberg Phenomenon of Disease," which segment is represented by the portion of the iceberg submerged below the water line?**  
A. Clinically diagnosed and symptomatic cases presenting to hospitals.  
B. Subclinical, unapparent, undiagnosed, or asymptomatic cases in the community.  
C. Fatal cases and tertiary complications.  
D. Patients admitted in intensive care units.

**Q3. Imposing a nationwide taxation policy on sugar-sweetened beverages and tobacco to prevent the emergence of behavioral risk factors is an example of:**  
A. Primordial prevention  
B. Primary prevention  
C. Secondary prevention  
D. Tertiary prevention

**Q4. Under Indian Public Health Standards (IPHS 2022), which facility serves as the First Referral Unit (FRU) providing 24/7 emergency obstetric care, surgical, and pediatric specialist services?**  
A. Ayushman Arogya Mandir (Sub-Health Centre)  
B. Primary Health Centre (PHC)  
C. Community Health Centre (CHC)  
D. Urban Health Post

**Q5. The Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY) under Ayushman Bharat provides financial risk protection for secondary and tertiary hospitalization up to:**  
A. ₹1 Lakh per individual per year  
B. ₹2 Lakhs per family per year  
C. ₹5 Lakhs per family per year  
D. ₹10 Lakhs per family per year

**Q6. Which of the following is considered a "distal / upstream" social determinant of health according to the Dahlgren-Whitehead model?**  
A. Blood pressure level  
B. Socioeconomic status and education level  
C. Genetic predisposition  
D. Daily caloric intake

### Domain II: Healthcare Management & Leadership

C. Policy, Oversight, Standards, Direction, Communication, Regulation, Benchmarking

**Q8. In Herzberg’s Two-Factor Motivation Theory, which of the following is classified as a "Motivator" (leading to true job satisfaction) rather than a "Hygiene" factor?**  
A. Basic salary

C. Sense of achievement and personal growth

**Q9. Under the Hersey-Blanchard Situational Leadership Model, which leadership style is most appropriate for a follower who is "unable and unwilling/insecure" (Readiness Level R1)?**

**Q10. In the Thomas-Kilmann Conflict Mode Instrument (TKI), an approach that is both highly assertive and highly cooperative, seeking a win-win solution, is termed:**  
A. Competing  
B. Collaborating

**Q11. When clinical and administrative teams hand over critical information during an emergency, the standardized structured communication framework SBAR stands for:**  
A. Symptoms, Baseline, Action, Results

**Q12. What distinguishes "Leadership" from "Management" in a hospital or public health department?**

### Domain III: Foundations of Epidemiology

**Q13. In epidemiology, what constitutes the classic "Epidemiological Triad" of infectious disease causation?**  
A. Doctor, Patient, Hospital  
B. Agent, Host, Environment  
C. Bacteria, Virus, Parasite

**Q14. The number of NEW cases of a disease occurring in a specified population over a defined period of time divided by the population at risk is termed:**  
A. Point Prevalence  
B. Period Prevalence  
C. Incidence  
D. Proportional Mortality Rate

**Q15. In a cohort study investigating smoking and lung cancer, the risk of lung cancer in smokers was 10 per 1,000, while in non-smokers it was 1 per 1,000. What is the Relative Risk (RR)?**  
A. 0.1  
B. 9.0

**Q16. Which observational study design is considered optimal for studying rare diseases with long latency periods?**  
A. Cross-Sectional Study  
B. Retrospective Case-Control Study  
C. Prospective Cohort Study  
D. Ecological Study

**Q17. A diagnostic test for hospital-acquired bacteremia correctly identifies 90 out of 100 truly infected patients. The Sensitivity of this test is:**  
A. 10%  
B. 90%  
C. 95%

**Q18. A systematic error in an epidemiological study resulting from differences in the accuracy of recalled past exposures between cases and controls is known as:**  
A. Selection bias

### Domain IV: Biostatistics & Quantitative Data Literacy

**Q19. The variable "Cancer Stage" recorded as Stage I, Stage II, Stage III, and Stage IV belongs to which scale of measurement?**  
A. Nominal scale  
B. Ordinal scale  
C. Interval scale  
D. Ratio scale

**Q20. When a distribution of hospital length of stay is severely positively skewed (skewed to the right by a few very long stay patients), which measure of central tendency is the most appropriate summary statistic?**  
A. Arithmetic Mean  
B. Median  
C. Mode  
D. Mid-range

**Q21. In a standard Gaussian (Normal) Distribution, approximately what percentage of observations fall within $\pm 2$ standard deviations ($\pm 2\sigma$) of the mean?**  
A. 50%  
B. 68%  
C. 95%  
D. 99.7%

**Q22. In hypothesis testing, committing a Type I error ($\alpha$) means:**  
A. Failing to reject the null hypothesis when it is actually false.  
B. Rejecting the null hypothesis when it is actually true (false positive).  
C. Selecting an inadequate sample size.  
D. Using a non-parametric test instead of a parametric test.

**Q23. A randomized trial reports that a new hospital disinfectant protocol reduces infection rates with a $p$-value of $0.01$ (with threshold $\alpha = 0.05$). What is the correct interpretation?**  
A. There is a 99% probability that the disinfectant is completely ineffective.  
B. The probability of observing this reduction (or more extreme) purely by chance under the null hypothesis is 1%.  
C. Exactly 1% of the patients got infected.  
D. The effect size is clinically insignificant.

**Q24. A study finds that the 95% Confidence Interval for the difference in average recovery days between two surgical techniques is $[-0.5 \text{ days to } +6.2 \text{ days}]$. What should the researcher conclude regarding statistical significance at the 5% level?**  
A. The difference is statistically significant because the upper limit is $>5$.  
B. The difference is NOT statistically significant because the interval crosses zero.  
C. The new surgical technique is definitely superior.  
D. The sample size was too large.

## Answer Key & Diagnostic Rationales

| Q# | Correct Answer | Competency Assessed | Explanatory Rationale |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **C** | Concept of Health | WHO 1948 definition explicitly recognizes physical, mental, and social dimensions beyond mere absence of pathology. |
| **2** | **B** | Iceberg Phenomenon | The visible tip represents clinical symptomatic cases; the vast submerged mass represents hidden subclinical/carrier cases. |
| **3** | **A** | Levels of Prevention | Primordial prevention acts on inhibiting the emergence of environmental and behavioral risk factors at the policy/societal level. |
| **4** | **C** | Health System Tiers | CHC (30 beds) is designated as the First Referral Unit (FRU) with core specialist services under IPHS. |
| **5** | **C** | Health Financing / AB | PM-JAY provides cashless secondary/tertiary cover up to ₹5 Lakhs per eligible family per year. |
| **6** | **B** | Social Determinants | Socioeconomic status and education represent foundational upstream societal determinants shaping downstream exposures. |
| **7** | **B** | Management Functions | Luther Gulick (1937): Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting, and Budgeting. |
| **8** | **C** | Motivation Theory | Achievement, recognition, and growth are true intrinsic Motivators; pay and work conditions are Hygiene factors. |
| **9** | **D** | Situational Leadership | Low competence and low commitment/confidence require high task direction with low relationship distraction (Directing - S1). |
| **10** | **B** | Conflict Resolution | Collaborating mode pairs high assertiveness with high cooperativeness to integrate all stakeholders' goals. |
| **11** | **B** | Communication Tools | SBAR stands for Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation—originating in high-reliability organizations. |
| **12** | **B** | Leadership vs Mgmt | Bennis/Kotter distinction: Managers manage complexity and process; leaders lead vision, alignment, and adaptive change. |
| **13** | **B** | Epidemiological Triad | The triad consists of the external Agent, susceptible Host, and Environment bringing them together. |
| **14** | **C** | Measures of Morbidity | Incidence specifically quantifies NEW cases occurring in a population at risk over time. |
| **15** | **C** | Risk Calculation | $RR = \text{Risk in exposed} / \text{Risk in unexposed} = 10/1 = 10.0$. |
| **16** | **B** | Study Design Selection | Case-control studies start with cases of the disease and look back, making them uniquely efficient for rare conditions. |
| **17** | **B** | Diagnostic Metrics | $\text{Sensitivity} = TP / (TP + FN) = 90 / 100 = 90\%$. |
| **18** | **B** | Epidemiological Bias | Differential memory accuracy between diseased cases and healthy controls is classic recall bias (information bias). |
| **19** | **B** | Measurement Scales | Cancer stages have an inherent natural order (I < II < III < IV) but unequal quantitative intervals, defining an Ordinal scale. |
| **20** | **B** | Descriptive Statistics | The Median is resistant to extreme outliers and is preferred for skewed data over the arithmetic mean. |
| **21** | **C** | Gaussian Curve | By the Empirical Rule of normal distributions, $\mu \pm 1\sigma \approx 68\%$, $\mu \pm 2\sigma \approx 95\%$, $\mu \pm 3\sigma \approx 99.7\%$. |
| **22** | **B** | Hypothesis Testing | Type I error ($\alpha$) is rejecting $H_0$ when $H_0$ is true (concluding an effect exists when it does not; false alarm). |
| **23** | **B** | Inferential Statistics | A $p$-value of $0.01$ means there is only a 1 in 100 chance of seeing this result by random sampling variation under $H_0$. |
| **24** | **B** | Confidence Intervals | For a difference between two means, a 95% CI spanning from negative to positive contains zero, indicating no statistically significant difference at $\alpha = 0.05$. |
| **25** | **B** | Research Questions | PICO: Population, Intervention/Exposure, Comparison/Control, Outcome. |
| **26** | **B** | Literature Search | Boolean grouping parentheses ensure the `OR` logic between diet and exercise is executed before intersecting with hypertension. |
| **27** | **D** | Evidence Hierarchy | Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses synthesize all available high-quality trial evidence at the top of the evidence pyramid. |
| **28** | **B** | Referencing Conventions | Vancouver style utilizes numeric in-text superscript or bracketed numbers matching an end-of-text citation list. |
| **29** | **A** | Bioethics Principles | Autonomy protects individual self-determination and mandates voluntary, informed, non-coerced consent. |
| **30** | **B** | Research Integrity | Fabrication is inventing data; Falsification is manipulating/omitting real data; Plagiarism is appropriating another's work. |
